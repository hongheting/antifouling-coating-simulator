## 2. UCIBIO: marinone-type natural-product biocide-free coating reduces mussel settlement
- Date: 2026-10-07
- Category: Technology
- Summary: After the crude extract PTM-346 was added to a biocide-free coating, mussel settlement fell by about 22%–33% across three formulations, and all mussels survived 10 days of exposure; antibiofilm performance under static conditions was limited, and longer dynamic and real-sea trials are needed.
- Source: https://ucibio.pt/news/ucibio-researchers-explore-eco-friendly-marinone-based-coatings-combat-marine-biofouling
- Relevance: Medium (a natural antifoulant in a formulation; effective against macrofouling but insufficient against biofilm)

## 3. New Zealand MPI commissions LWP to survey shipowners' views on biocide-free antifouling
- Date: 2026-10-07
- Category: Regulation
- Summary: Land Water People is running an anonymous questionnaire commissioned by MPI, covering effectiveness, cost, maintenance, cleaning habits and barriers to adoption, to assess the feasibility of the domestic fleet switching to biocide-free antifouling.
- Source: https://www.yachtingnz.org.nz/news/survey-boat-owners-invited-share-views-biocide-free-antifouling
- Relevance: Medium (could influence New Zealand's policy stance toward biocide-free coatings)

## 5. Progress in Organic Coatings: hyperbranched crosslinked amphiphilic coating combines three antifouling mechanisms
- Date: 2026-10-09
- Category: Technology
- Summary: Hyperbranched polycaprolactone chemically crosslinked with an isothiazolinone-functionalised amphiphilic polymer achieves anti-adhesion, controlled biocide release and controlled degradation simultaneously; compared with physical blending, cumulative biocide release fell to about 1/20.
- Source: https://www.european-coatings.com/news/coatings-technologies/hyperbranched-coating-combines-three-antifouling-mechanisms/
- Relevance: High (a formulation strategy of chemically anchoring the biocide to reduce release)

## Weekly takeaway
Biocide-free and low-release routes advanced on both the academic and policy fronts: New Zealand began systematically assessing biocide-free antifouling, and studies of natural products and chemically anchored biocides multiplied. Physical methods such as UV-C combined with coatings also produced real-sea panel data.
