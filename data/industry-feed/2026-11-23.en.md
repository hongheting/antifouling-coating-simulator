## 1. Hempel showcases Hempaguard X7 at METSTRADE, with independent life-cycle assessment data
- Date: 2026-11-14
- Category: Product
- Summary: At the METSTRADE show in Amsterdam on 17–19 November, Hempel included Hempaguard X7 in its superyacht sustainability route (booth 02.423) and cited a comparative life-cycle assessment by the Water Revolution Foundation: compared with the conventional Hempel Mille NCT 71880 scheme, endpoint-level environmental impact improved by 31.5% (this figure is an aggregated indicator under the specific assumptions of that assessment, not a fuel-saving rate, and cannot be read directly as fuel savings). ## 2. GIT graphene foul-release coating + onboard robotic grooming completes one year of in-service operation
- Date: 2026-11-15
- Category: Technology
- Summary: An industry analysis published on 15 November reviewed a case disclosed by GIT Coatings: a gas carrier using a graphene-based foul-release coating, together with an onboard robotic grooming system, completed a full year of continuous commercial operation, during which 7 underwater inspections and 3 grooming sessions were carried out; according to GIT's disclosure, power performance after dry-docking improved by about 6% against the previous premium antifouling baseline, added power growth over the year was kept within about 2% (including more than 180 days idle), each grooming session restored 3–5% efficiency, and no hard fouling appeared over the year and the coating showed no measurable degradation. All of these figures are from a single-vessel case disclosed by the company and have not been independently verified by a third party; the underlying case study was reported at the HullPIC conference in early 2026. BW Epic Kosan has expanded its adoption of the system across multiple LPG carriers.
- Source: https://maritimeng.net/news/graphene-grooming-trial-signals-new-economics-for-hull-performance
- Relevance: Medium (the "continuous hull management" model of biocide-free coating + active grooming now has its first full year of in-service data, but it is a single vessel with company-stated figures, and multi-vessel, multi-year data with independent verification is needed before it can serve as a basis for selection)

## 3. Neptune Robotics partners with Louis Dreyfus to trial AI hull-cleaning robots in Singapore
- Date: 2026-11-17
- Category: Technology
- Summary: Louis Dreyfus Company (LDC) and Neptune Robotics have entered a partnership to run robotic hull-cleaning trials under the Maritime and Port Authority of Singapore (MPA) innovation programme for underwater hull inspection and cleaning, lasting 18 months; LDC will open its fleet of more than 300 chartered vessels for in-service testing, evaluating the inspection and cleaning performance of the AI-driven robots and their impact on fuel consumption and emissions, with results expected in 2028. ## 4. AkzoNobel's International brand launches biocide-free yacht antifouling Micron ReGen
- Date: 2026-11-17
- Category: Product
- Summary: On 17 November at METSTRADE, AkzoNobel launched Micron ReGen under its International brand, described as the first yacht antifouling to use "Surface Renewal Technology": a microporous surface layer with both hydrophilic and hydrophobic character continuously renews the surface after immersion to hinder attachment, with no biocide required. ## 5. Chinese patent published for a low-migration siloxane-grafted acrylic antifouling coating
- Date: 2026-11-17
- Category: Technology
- Summary: On 17 November, the China National Intellectual Property Administration published an antifouling coating invention patent (applicant: Luo Juan): a di-epoxy-terminated siloxane is prepared by hydrosilylation and reacted with an aminosilane to obtain a "self-anchoring siloxane", which is then copolymerised with acrylates to give a siloxane-grafted acrylic resin; its design goal is to chemically anchor the siloxane segments within the resin network, reducing the loss of low surface energy caused by silicone oil migrating to the coating surface, while the acrylic resin provides adhesion and mechanical strength. A patent publication is only a disclosure of a technical solution, with no real-sea antifouling validation data.
- Source: https://www.jishuxx.com/coating-adhesive-device/97355.html
- Relevance: Low (a domestic patent development on the silicone–acrylic hybrid route, related to low-surface-energy antifouling formulation thinking, but it is only a patent publication, with no performance or real-sea evidence)

## 7. Nine shipping associations jointly call for a global agreement on the IMO net-zero framework; MEPC 85 opens on 30 November
- Date: 2026-11-18
- Category: Regulation
- Summary: On 18 November, nine associations — BIMCO, CLIA, IBIA, ICS, INTERCARGO, INTERFERRY, INTERTANKO, IPTA and the World Shipping Council — issued a joint statement urging governments to reach "workable, enforceable and stable" global rules on the IMO net-zero framework, stressing that only global rules can avoid fragmentation of regional rules and double charging. MEPC 85 will be held in London from 30 November to 3 December, but an adoption vote is not on its agenda; the second extraordinary session, which adjourned in October 2025, is planned to resume on 4 December to vote on the MARPOL Annex VI amendments, and whether it resumes must first be confirmed by MEPC 85. The draft sets, for 2028–2030, two tiers of remedial-unit floor prices at USD 100 and USD 380 per tonne of CO₂ equivalent, and the entry-into-force date of 1 May 2028 remains in square brackets, undecided.
- Source: https://www.info.boaton.fr/post/mepc-85-what-is-actually-at-stake-in-london-in-early-december?lang=en
- Relevance: Medium (if global carbon pricing is implemented, it will directly raise the compliance cost of increased hull resistance, strengthening the economic value of efficient antifouling and hull performance management; but whether a vote can take place on 4 December is still uncertain, and the United States and other countries are clearly opposed)

## 8. Paper on a fluorine-free laser superhydrophobic coating published: femtosecond laser + point-like organic–inorganic reinforcement
- Date: 2026-11-18
- Category: Technology
- Summary: A study published in Progress in Organic Coatings (article 110466): 7075 aluminium alloy was textured by femtosecond laser into a biomimetic cauliflower-like multi-scale structure; KH550 silane formed an Al-O-Si interfacial anchoring layer; KH570-modified ZnO and low-solids E44 epoxy built a point-like reinforcing skeleton; and octadecyltrimethoxysilane provided the low-surface-energy outer layer, with no fluorinated modifiers used anywhere in the process. The optimised coating had a water contact angle of 163.4° and a sliding angle of 2.1°, remained superhydrophobic after 240 abrasion cycles and 200 tape-peeling cycles, achieved an anticorrosion efficiency of 99.86% after 100 hours of immersion in 3.5% NaCl (by electrochemical impedance), delayed icing at −20 °C from 181 seconds to 857 seconds, and reduced ice adhesion strength from 142 kPa to 34 kPa. Antifouling is only a potential application direction; no marine organism attachment tests were carried out.
- Source: https://www.coatings-tech.com/technology-formulation/coating-systems-technologies/fluorine-free-laser-coating-protects-aluminium-alloy-durably/
- Relevance: Low (the durability thinking behind fluorine-free superhydrophobic interfaces is relevant to antifouling surface design, but the subject is a laboratory aluminium-alloy specimen with no real-sea antifouling evidence, indirectly related to hull antifouling formulation selection)

## 9. EU formally decides not to renew medetomidine (Selektope); countdown begins for antifouling paints containing the substance to exit the EU market
- Date: 2026-11-20
- Category: Regulation
- Summary: Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/2567, adopted by the European Commission on 19 November and published in the Official Journal on 20 November, formally does not renew the approval of medetomidine (trade name Selektope) as a biocidal active substance for product type 21 (antifouling products); the decision takes effect on 10 December 2026, and the accompanying Decision (EU) 2026/2566 simultaneously repeals the earlier extension that had prolonged the approval to 31 December 2026; from 8 June 2027, articles treated with, or intentionally incorporating, that substance may no longer be placed on the EU market. The decision rests on the European Chemicals Agency's May 2024 opinion concerning endocrine-disrupting properties: no safe exposure level could be established, nor could the risks of representative products be deemed acceptable. The decision also lists four chemical alternatives considered adequately available for commercial shipping and yachts: DCOIT, copper thiocyanate, cuprous oxide and tralopyril. Vessels already coated with Selektope coatings are unaffected and may continue to call at EU ports; I-Tech states that the EU market accounts for about 4% of its sales and that about 95% of the global commercial fleet can continue to use Selektope-containing coatings (company figures). This is the formal EU outcome following last issue's public consultation by the New Zealand EPA on the same active substance.
- Source: https://tower.useforesight.io/news/eu-medetomidine-antifouling-non-renewal-2026
- Relevance: High (the split in Selektope access is now formally settled: the EU has closed the renewal by legislative decision and set a clear market-exit timetable, so the regional compliance risk for biocide-containing routes has shifted from "voting intention" to "decision in force", directly affecting the European market and formulation substitution choices for products containing this active substance)

## Weekly takeaway
This week's theme is "biocide-free routes accelerating into products, biocide-containing routes accelerating into restriction": at METSTRADE, AkzoNobel launched Micron ReGen, a directly recoatable biocide-free yacht paint; Hempel backed an existing hybrid system with an independent life-cycle assessment; and GIT's graphene coating + robotic grooming produced a full year of in-service data — three lines that together point to hull performance management that does not rely on biocides moving from concept towards purchasable, applicable products and services. Meanwhile, the EU formally closed the medetomidine renewal with Decision (EU) 2026/2567 and set a market-exit line of 8 June 2027, in contrast with New Zealand's consultation in the previous issue: the access map for the same active substance is differentiating by jurisdiction. At the regulatory level, the nine shipping associations jointly called for global carbon rules, and with MEPC 85 and the resumed-session vote on 4 December approaching, every increment of hull resistance may translate directly into compliance cost — the shared economic backdrop for efficient antifouling and active hull management alike. On the domestic patent front, incremental routes such as silicone hybrids still predominate, with no comparable real-sea-level breakthrough seen.
