## 1. Jotun publishes DNV-verified data from its PortPIC 2026 study
- Date: 2026-11-02
- Category: Technology
- Summary: On 2 November, Jotun released the verified-data basis of its PortPIC 2026 study (the underlying article was published on 26 October and was covered in the previous issue): based on the ISO 19030 method and real-ship operating data, DNV verified, across 33 vessels using Hull Skating Solutions, an average of 3.5 years with no measurable speed loss and hulls kept always clean; one large container ship cut CO₂ by 17.7% per year, about 17,300 tonnes. A second study showed that controlled surface treatment and application of sea chests can improve antifouling performance. The study also accounted for the footprint of the coating, the robot and the onboard station itself, concluding that the net climate benefit is positive. The emission-reduction figures above are company figures on a DNV verification basis, not measurements made by this simulator.

## 2. Paper on an oyster-shell bio-based dual-temperature antifouling coating published
- Date: 2026-11-03
- Category: Technology
- Summary: The November 2026 issue of Coatings (vol. 16, issue 11, article 1304) published a study by a Taiwanese team: a waterborne acrylic coating combining oyster shell powder calcined at 600 °C (15 wt% of dry solids) with AZO@SiO₂ core–shell nanoparticles; in 12-month real-sea panel immersion at Anping Port, macrofouling was visibly lower than on the blank control, though local deposition remained; under 100 W/m² laboratory illumination, the coating surface rose to 42.1±1.8 °C within 90 seconds (control 31.2±0.8 °C); in a 28-day leaching test, Cu, Zn, Sn and Fe were all below detection limits; the volatile-amine repellence and photothermal antifouling mechanisms remain hypotheses, and the authors explicitly did not carry out controlled attachment-biology validation.
- Source: https://doi.org/10.3390/coatings16111304
- Relevance: Medium (a copper-free waterborne antifouling idea based on waste valorisation, benchmarkable against biocide-free formulation routes; the unverified mechanism and limited real-sea effect are important caveats)

## 3. New Zealand EPA opens public consultation on Selektope imports and Selektope-containing yacht paint
- Date: 2026-11-05
- Category: Regulation
- Summary: On 5 November, the New Zealand Environmental Protection Authority (EPA) opened public consultations on two applications, closing on 17 December: I-Tech's application to import or manufacture the new active substance Selektope (medetomidine) (APP204449), and Altex Coatings' application for its Altex Ablative Antifoul (TFS) range of antifouling paints containing that substance (APP204450). If approved, Selektope would be limited to professional use in commercial settings for manufacturing antifouling paint, while the finished paints are intended for both professional and non-professional users; the EPA will assess the human-health and New Zealand environmental risks and benefits. This is Selektope's market-access progress in another jurisdiction after EU member states voted on 3 November to support non-renewal.
- Source: https://www.epa.govt.nz/news-and-alerts/latest-news/epa-seeks-feedback-on-applications-to-stop-barnacles-on-boats/
- Relevance: High (the direction of Selektope access in New Zealand after its setback in the EU directly affects regional market expectations for products containing this active substance)
- Note: Pending review (regional market-access wording for Selektope-containing products on the shortlist; ratings unchanged)

## 6. Paper on a kaolin-reinforced superhydrophobic epoxy anticorrosion coating published
- Date: 2026-11-11
- Category: Technology
- Summary: The November 2026 issue of Coatings (vol. 16, issue 11, article 1339) published a study of a three-layer superhydrophobic anticorrosion coating: a kaolin-reinforced epoxy primer + elastic adhesive interlayer + hydrophobic SiO₂ topcoat, with a water contact angle of 157.8° and a sliding angle of 2.1°; superhydrophobicity was retained after 19 m of abrasion or 200 tape-peeling cycles; in 3.5% NaCl, the corrosion current density fell to 7.9×10⁻¹² A·cm⁻², with a protection efficiency of about 99.33% after 15 days of immersion; the structure remained intact through salt-spray, immersion, outdoor and UV-ageing tests and antifouling self-cleaning tests; the antifouling evidence was only a qualitative laboratory self-cleaning test, with no real-sea antifouling validation.
- Source: https://doi.org/10.3390/coatings16111339
- Relevance: Low (mainly anticorrosion and self-cleaning interface design; the antifouling evidence is limited to qualitative laboratory tests, indirectly related to hull antifouling formulation selection)

## Weekly takeaway
This week, Selektope access diverged: after EU member states voted to support non-renewal, New Zealand's EPA promptly opened a public consultation on the substance and on yacht paints containing it, making the direction of regional access a key variable for biocide-containing routes. On the copper-free side, the papers on the oyster-shell bio-based coating and the kaolin superhydrophobic coating both offered laboratory highlights alongside explicit caveats at the real-sea and mechanism levels, a reminder that formulation selection should still be governed by real-sea evidence. On the supply side, coating automation landed in a concentrated way across domestic shipyards — laser cleaning equipment released, four-robot linked painting, electrostatic spraying entering dry-dock repair — which, together with Jotun's DNV-verified data, pushes "verifiable hull performance" to the fore; leading manufacturers' group results continued to grow, and domestic package orders expanded in step.
