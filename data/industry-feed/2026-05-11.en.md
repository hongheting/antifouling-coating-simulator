## 1. I-Tech releases barnacle fouling risk white paper: coating selection is a key risk factor
- Date: 2026-05-11
- Category: Technology
- Summary: The white paper is based on Safinah Group dry-dock inspection data: of 686 vessels, more than one third entered dock with barnacle coverage above 10%, and nearly one fifth above 20%; fouling-release coatings are more prone to barnacle settlement during long idle periods, in warm water and low-speed service, and at areas of mechanical coating damage. An experimental fouling-release system containing Selektope still showed resistance to barnacle settlement where the coating was damaged.
- Source: https://selektope.com/new-whitepaper-supports-ship-owners-in-navigating-rising-barnacle-biofouling-risks-ahead-of-tightening-biofouling-management-rules/
- Relevance: High (maps directly onto the SPC vs FRC selection logic and risk assessment for warm-water, long-idle routes; note that it is a vendor white paper)

## 3. 725th Research Institute completes silicone biomimetic drag-reduction antifouling coating on "Rui Lu Hao"
- Date: 2026-05-14
- Category: Product
- Summary: The vessel trades along China's southern, eastern and northern coasts and left dock in early May; the report stresses that nutrient-rich nearshore waters and long low-speed berthing mean both fouling pressure and coating wear are higher than in ocean service, and this application is seen as a validation of silicone biomimetic coatings on high-frequency nearshore vessels.
- Source: https://www.chinacoatings.com.cn/news/show.php?itemid=11224
- Relevance: High (a silicone system used in high-fouling nearshore, low-speed conditions; very relevant as a reference for route-based selection)

## 4. Quebec PAINTS project: multi-site panel testing of biocide-free xerogel and silicone coatings
- Date: 2026-05-12
- Category: Technology
- Summary: Research teams in Quebec and France tested 8 xerogel and silicone coatings at sites including Rimouski, Sept-Îles, Toulon, Lorient and Réunion; some biocide-free coatings performed slightly worse than biocide-containing paints but showed real antifouling effect and are environmentally safe, durability still needs improvement, and they are not yet at the stage of large-scale commercialisation.
- Source: https://www.scientifique-en-chef.gouv.qc.ca/en/research-impact/reinventing-anti-fouling-paint-to-support-marine-biodiversity/
- Relevance: Medium (comparisons across waters from cold to tropical; a reference for the regional applicability of biocide-free formulations)

## Weekly takeaway
The common theme this week is selecting coatings by operating condition: the I-Tech white paper points out that fouling-release coatings are prone to barnacle settlement under idle, low-speed and warm-water conditions, while domestically silicone biomimetic coatings are being promoted on high-fouling nearshore vessels. Biocide-free systems proved usable in multi-site panel tests, but durability remains their weak point.
