## 1. Progress in Organic Coatings: curcumin-grafted SiO₂/PDMS nanocomposite antifouling coating passes a 180-day real-sea test
- Date: 2026-06-24
- Category: Technology
- Summary: Lei et al. used RAFT polymerisation to graft curcumin and N-vinylpyrrolidone onto xanthate-functionalised silica, added it as an active filler to hydroxyl-terminated PDMS, and combined three mechanisms — self-polishing, slow release of curcumin through ester-bond hydrolysis, and low-surface-energy fouling release; antifouling performance remained stable in a 180-day real-sea test.
- Source: https://www.european-coatings.com/news/coatings-technologies/curcumin-grafted-nanocomposite-enables-long-lasting-marine-coating/
- Relevance: Medium (the multi-mechanism approach combining natural-product slow release with PDMS is a useful reference for formulation design)

## 2. Vessels stranded in the Strait of Hormuz heavily fouled; cleaning demand surges
- Date: 2026-06-23
- Category: Company
- Summary: Around 600 vessels have been stranded in the Persian Gulf for months because of the strait closure, their hulls covered with barnacles, mussels and algae; large numbers of divers are needed to clean them before they can resume sailing, and a BIMCO representative said cleaning costs have risen to five figures in USD per vessel, with care also needed during cleaning to avoid damaging the antifouling coating.
- Source: https://www.cnn.com/2026/06/23/business/barnacles-strait-of-hormuz
- Relevance: High (long anchorage in warm water is the most severe operating condition for antifouling coatings, directly relevant to selection for Middle East routes)

## 3. IMCA warns of rising risk in underwater ship husbandry as Hormuz traffic resumes
- Date: 2026-06-25
- Category: Regulation
- Summary: The International Marine Contractors Association notes that demand for underwater ship husbandry (including hull cleaning) is surging as the strait reopens, while such work is among the most dangerous areas of commercial diving; the association calls for using qualified contractors, surface-supplied diving rather than SCUBA, and following the IMCA D082 guidelines.
- Source: https://safety4sea.com/imca-warns-of-rising-risk-in-underwater-ship-husbandry-as-strait-of-hormuz-activity-resumes/
- Relevance: Low (focused on operational safety, with only an indirect effect on formulations)

## Weekly takeaway
The large-scale stranding of vessels in the Strait of Hormuz has become an extreme test of antifouling coatings under long static exposure in warm water, and cleaning demand and costs are both soaring. Academically, a multi-mechanism coating combining natural-product slow release with PDMS fouling release has passed a relatively long real-sea validation.
